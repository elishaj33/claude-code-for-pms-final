The short version

Two changes shipped together on 12 August. The system punished slow phones, and four responders paid for it.

Before 4.2Ping wait 90s
Weights: closeness 0.45 · acceptance 0.40 · skills 0.15
12 Aug · 4.2 shipsPing wait 60s
Weights: closeness 0.60 · acceptance 0.25 · skills 0.15
12-18 AugEveryone misses more pings. Each miss costs the same as saying no (−0.12).
By 29 AugFour responders sit at the floor and are almost never asked. Tickets stay 3-4× normal.
STATED

The wait dropped to 60s, the weights moved, a miss and a refusal cost the same, and scores only climb when someone takes a ping.

INFERRED

The shorter wait made missed pings common, and the ranking turned early misses into a lasting loss of pings for the four.

NOT YET KNOWN

Why those four missed so much more than the other twelve. We have no response-time data and Wen Li hasn't confirmed the ranking behaviour.

The previous "it's mostly seasonal" read doesn't hold for the concentration: the drop is in four people, not spread across everyone.

Module 4 · X-Ray Vision · the rule in the code

The code treats a slow phone like a refusal, and gives no way back up except being asked.

Module 4 · my prompt 6tell me more about the score for each responder and how that was hurting the four responders who lost pings over time. What about their data and this code combined, will answer how the routing or score rating should be adjusted
What it foundA taken ping adds +0.08; a turn-down or a miss takes −0.12; the score floors at 0 and lives in memory only. A responder needs about 60% of pings taken just to hold steady. Replaying the real pings through those rules, the four hit zero between 21 and 29 Aug and nobody else fell below 0.28. Misses were 77% of the four's lost points after 12 Aug; out-of-area pings, which were never taken before 4.2, were another 51%.

An approximation: real outcomes, but not real travel times, and it assumes no restart.

What would change this view

Response times mostly under 30s would weaken the "wait" story. Wen saying the acceptance window is long, or that proximity dominates anyway, would change the ranking part. September data showing the four recovering would change both.

What the tickets say

Two thirds are "phone never goes off", one third "gone before I could answer", which matches the one-third wait story. The two-thirds group is still unexplained. Dot and Kip filed no tickets even though their responders lost about two thirds of their pings, so ticket counts say who writes in, not who is affected.

What isn't the cause

Seasonality (the drop is concentrated, not spread). Low prior acceptance. Distance. Supply: it only reads the availability record, and no path to the ping drop was found.

What we'd build

Make a miss cheap, don't count out-of-area misses, and give the four a fresh start.

Try it: one responder starts at 50% and misses pings in a row

Same score rules as the code. Today a miss costs 0.12; with the change it costs 0.03.

What changes
  • A missed ping costs −0.03, not −0.12. A slow phone isn't a "no".
  • A miss on an out-of-area ping doesn't count against anyone.
  • A one-off fresh start puts the four back at 0.5, and they're told why.
  • Handlers get a "gone quiet" alert after 7 days with no pings, and a read-only "who is next" view.
  • Dispatch starts recording answer times.

In the replay of real pings

Cheaper misses alone lift the four to about 0.5-0.6. Ignoring out-of-area pings alone does the same. Both together reach about 0.84. A fresh start alone, or easing scores back toward 0.5 over time, doesn't help.

Deliberately left alone

The 60s wait and the weights wait for response times from Ravi. No quotas, no handler control of routing, and nothing that assumes who anyone is.

How it doesn't happen quietly again

Four layers: fix the rule, see the problem, measure it, and review before it ships.

1 · Fix the rule

Cheaper misses, ignore out-of-area misses, one-off fresh start. A slow phone no longer costs a responder their place in line.

2 · See it

Gone-quiet alert after 7 days, answer-time logging, a log of score changes, and a read-only "who is next" view for handlers. Today none of this exists.

3 · Measure it

Baselines cut at the release date, not Mondays. Targets: zero responders with no pings in 14 days; the four back toward 10-14 a week within 4 weeks; acceptance and time-to-accept no worse.

4 · Review first

Run the brief checklist on every brief. Replay real pings through any ranking change before it ships. Tell the engineering manager before changing config.
